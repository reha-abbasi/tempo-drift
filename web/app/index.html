          Version 16 · coins in glass · new name

          Tempo Drift

          Rebuilt around the Golden Hour Realism style: real sunlight, shadows, reflections and textured buildings in every city. Three eras instead of four, back to three lanes, and new things to do: tap to shatter glass on the beat, grab power-ups, chase missions and a daily gift.

          What’s new

          • A name: Tempo Drift

            You drift through time (Past, Present, Future) to the tempo of the music. “Tempo” means both time and the speed of the beat.

          • Coins in glass: tap to break them open

            Some coins now come sealed in a glass case. Tap the screen as you reach one to break it open and catch the coin. A thin ring closes in on the case to show the moment: tap right as it lands for PERFECT (300 points), close to it for GOOD (200), a little early for 100. If you don’t break it, you lose that coin and its note, but not a life. A tap with nothing to break is a misfire and resets your combo. The old glass walls across the lanes are gone. Later episodes have more glass coins (about 3 in episode 1, up to 20 in episode 5).

          • Calmer coins

            No more glow around the coins: they are plain gold that catches the light, with only a slight beat and a small, quiet sparkle when you catch one. The line that links the coins is dimmer too.

          • A new main menu

            The city and your car now fill the screen, like the title screens of Subway Surfers and Alto’s Adventure. Your coins and stars sit at the top, with a gear for settings. Pick an era with the PAST / PRESENT / FUTURE tabs, the arrows, or a swipe across the screen. One big PLAY starts the right episode for you: Flight School the first time, then the next episode you haven’t cleared. EPISODES opens the full list, ENDLESS starts Infinite mode, and Garage, Missions and Scores are three tiles at the bottom (a red dot on Missions means one is more than half done).

          • Settings in one place

            Sound, haptics, Endless difficulty, replaying Flight School and dev mode moved off the menu into the settings panel.

          • A tap never changes lane

            Before, a tap where the finger slid about 2 mm could count as a swipe. Now anything under a clear swipe is a tap, and a tap only fires the pulse.

          • Smoother on slower phones

            If the phone can’t keep up, the game steps down: lower resolution first, then no shadows, then no wet-street mirror. The phone remembers the level it settles on.

          • Hops and dives last one note

            Going up or down is now always quick: a single coin above or below the skyway, then straight back down. There are no more rows of coins up there to stay with, and holding your finger no longer keeps you up. To keep the up-and-down moves in every song, the highest (or lowest) note becomes a hop (or dive) every couple of bars, a bit more often in later episodes. Same in all three eras.

          • The realistic look, everywhere

            Sunlight casts shadows from the buildings, traffic and your car. Glass towers and car paint reflect the sky. Brick, stone and window frames have depth, the asphalt is real asphalt, and the street sits at ground level with sidewalks and trees. At night in the Future the wet street mirrors the city. The arcade glow (purple lanes, big rings, speed streaks) is turned way down, and the middle height is now a warm white.

          • Three eras, each with its own music

            The Past has a swing band (walking bass, brushes, and a lead per city: saloon piano, oud, accordion, clarinet, trumpet). The Present has bright pop. The Future is a dystopian cyberpunk night with rain and smog, and a darker, industrial sound: a growling bass and metallic hits on the backbeat.

          • Three lanes, swipe controls

            Swipe left and right to change lanes, up to hop, down to dive. The coins’ lanes still follow the melody, spread wider so every city keeps you moving (about 21–36 moves a minute, more each episode).

          • Power-ups (from Subway Surfers)

            Now and then a coin is replaced by a power-up. Magnet pulls in coins from the other lanes and heights for 10 seconds. Shield puts a bubble around the car that absorbs your next crash. 2× doubles your score for 12 seconds. Active ones show at the top right.

          • Missions and a daily gift (from Jetpack Joyride and Crossy Road)

            MISSIONS in the menu shows three goals, like catching 150 coins, breaking 5 coins out of glass or finishing an episode without crashing. Each pays coins when done, and a harder one replaces it. Opening the game each day gives a gift: 100 coins, then 50 more for every day in a row, up to 400.

          • Flight School

            Each move is shown only once, and the world goes into slow motion instead of stopping until you make it. It now teaches the pulse too.

          Model test

          The real game logic runs without graphics while simulated players play every episode in all three eras, 6 runs per cell (perfect: 3). They tap to shatter glass as well as swiping. Average: 0.2 s reaction, 0.16 s per swipe, 2 wrong moves in 100. Casual: 0.27 s and 0.2 s, never holds a swipe, 4 in 100. Both make more mistakes when rushed.           Perfect play gets all 5 stars in all 15 cities, so every episode can be fully cleared. Moves are swipes a minute for the average player (taps on glass come on top). Hops are one-note hops and dives per episode, and the car is never off the skyway for more than about half a second.

          Kept: the garage and its five cars, Flight School, the 5-star system, Infinite mode, the high-score board, dev mode, haptics, the menu music, the studio intro, the skyway hops and dives, the edge bounce, the melody cutting out on a miss, pause with a count-in and the silent-switch fix. The previous version (stylized look, four eras, five lanes) is saved as a file in the project folder.
